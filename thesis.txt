This paper needs to take a different tone.

The purpose of this paper is to explore the errors in muskingum routing and their mitigation options which determines the minimum feature set
required for applying muskingum routing to large numerical models such as national and global scale options in circulation today.

It will explore this problem by doing the following:

1. mathematical treatment of the sources of potential errors.
    - improper parameterization of k, x, and dt.
2. Why these errors become prevalent in large scale models.
    - analysis of national and global scale hydrography datasets, namely NHDPlus, HydroSHEDS, NWM Next Gen, TDX-Hydro
    - estimated incidence of problematic reach sizes in these datasets.
3. Theoretical and mathematical exploration of options available to avoid these errors.
4. Exploration of how two routing algorithms, t-route and RAPID, avoid these errors, if they do.
5. Exploring quantitatively through test cases:
    - how these errors manifest in models under real forcing conditions.
    - the significance in the difference in answers when these errors are not addressed.
    - the efficacy of mitigation options to avoid these errors.
6. Discussion of the implications of:
    - the prevalence of these errors in large scale models.
    - required minimum feature set to mitigate muskingum errors.
    - best practices for large models using muskingum routing.

This paper will need to have a literature review component and mathematical treatment component.

this paper needs to lead to a few major outcomes as a result of the muskingum exploration set in the hypothesis:
- introduce the river-route code whose numerical stabilization procedure implements the necessary guard rails.
- benchmark the performance and accuracy of the river-route code from strictly numerical perspective, not a calibration or gauge comparison perspective.

This paper should clearly express that it is not intended to be a model calibration exercise, but purely a numerical exploration of how it must
be carried out.

Some of the conclusions should include at least:
1. Do no clamp flows to zero when they go negative. this is a band-aid to hide invalid results that introduces errors that are correctable in other ways.
2. Do not modify the hydrography purely for the purpose of making a simpler topological network. There are routing algorithmic means to address real life situations.
3. It is better to have fewer, longer reaches that can be validly subdivided into short reaches than to have many short reaches which require assumptions to handle.
4. the correct definition of the Q solved for by muskingum is the flow at the next time step that, when averaged with the flow at the previous time step,
    represents the average flow over the interval. This is not the same thing as the instantaneous discharge at the end of the time step. This is not the same
    thing as the average flow over the time step on its own. This is a subtle but important distinction that is often overlooked in the literature and in practice.
5. The muskingum method is a numerical approximation of the continuity equation and inventory equation.

Discussion items include:
- given the choice of dt, model builders often take the approach that dt is purely a matter of how many time steps they want to store a result for and assume
    that higher resolution on the time step is always better. You should pick a dt that is appropriate for the reach's travel time. This is a Currant like condition
    that is often overlooked in the literature and in practice.
- You should not assume you need to pick a higher resolution time step to ensure valid results. You should consider the temporal resolution of the forcing data which
    is quite often 1 hourly accumulation at the highest resolution when talking about continental or larger scale models and often 3 hourly or lower resolution.
