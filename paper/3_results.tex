Figure~\ref{fig:hydrography-travel-times} shows the cumulative distribution of reach lengths as equivalent travel times, $k$,
in the hydrography products. The travel time is calculated as $k = Length / velocity$ assuming constant 0.5 m/s velocity. Across all datasets,
the length of reaches spans multiple orders of magnitude. The median reach length spans around 1.3~km in HydroSHEDS v2 to 7.4~km in GRIT. The
analogous travel time given the assumed velocity is equivalently around 43 minutes to 4.1 hours. The combined distribution is used as representative
of trends that apply to individual datasets. An equivalent figure for each dataset individually is provided in the appendix section~
\ref{sec:appendix-hydrography-results}.

    \centering
    \includegraphics{../figures/hydrofabric_census_distributions}
    \caption{Cumulative distribution of reach travel times in the hydrography products.}

\end{figure}

The histogram is made combining the estimated travel times shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hydrography-travel-times} with the validity conditions in
Equation~\ref{eq:validity-conditions}. The $\Delta t$ bins are on the x-axis with the same assumed constant velocity of 0.5 m/s and the
corresponding $x = 0.25$. Figure~\ref{fig:dt_window_histogram} contains a histogram of the relative percentage of rivers across datasets which has
all positive Muskingum coefficients for a given $\Delta t$. The $\Delta t$ which is valid for the largest percentage of rivers is around 1 hour.
These findings are incompatible with the common practice assuming that smaller time steps correlates with more accurate results. The NWM uses a
5-minute time step \citep{read2023}, GRADES-hydroDL uses a 30-minute time step \citep{yang2025}, RFS used a 15-minute time step \citep{hales2022}.
Various published modeling studies using RAPID have used 15-minute time steps in the San Antonio and Guadalupe basins of Texas using the NHDPlus
\citep{david2011, emery2020} and the western United States \citep{david2019}, 30-minute time steps in France \citep{david2011hp} and the Mississippi
River basin \citep{david2015}, and a 3-hour time step globally for GRADES \citep{lin2019}.
